Why this matters now

1 in 5 UK adults is disabled.

If your checkout cannot be completed with a keyboard, or your product images have no descriptions, that is revenue walking away before it is ever a legal problem.

There is also a deadline behind you rather than ahead of you. The European Accessibility Act has applied since 28 June 2025, and 2026 is the first full year national authorities are actively supervising against it. It reaches any UK business an EU consumer can buy from, and member-state penalties run from around €60,000 to €900,000.

Closer to home, the Equality Act 2010 has required UK service providers to make reasonable adjustments for years — and that has always included websites.

We are not lawyers and this is not legal advice. What we do is show you, concretely, where your site does not meet the standard those laws point at.

How it works

  1. You send us your URL and tell us which pages matter most — usually your homepage, a product or service page, and your checkout or enquiry form.
  2. We audit. Our own testing engine runs across every page at desktop and mobile sizes, then we do the manual passes automation cannot do: keyboard-only navigation, reading order, heading structure, form grouping and error handling.
  3. You get the report. Findings ranked by real user impact, each with the exact element, the WCAG criterion, and the fix — written so your developer can act on it immediately, and summarised so you can understand it without one.
  4. You fix, we verify. On the Compliance Pack we re-audit after 30 days to confirm the fixes landed.

What makes our audit different

We test WCAG 2.2, not 2.1

Including target size (2.5.8) and focus appearance — criteria most audits written before 2024 do not check at all.

We separate facts from guesses

Confirmed failures are labelled separately from flagged risks. Where we cannot judge something honestly — contrast over a background image, for instance — we say so instead of padding the report.

We never recommend overlays

Accessibility overlay widgets are widely regarded as ineffective and have themselves attracted complaints. We will tell you to remove one if you have it.

Questions

How are you so much cheaper than an agency?

UK agencies commonly charge £4,950 and up per site, because they are billing senior consultant hours end to end. We built our own testing engine, so the mechanical part of the work takes minutes rather than days, and a human reviews and writes up the result. Same standard, far less time, and we pass that on. It is also why we can afford the no-issues-no-fee guarantee.

Will this make me legally compliant?

It tells you precisely where you currently fail WCAG 2.2 Level AA and how to fix each issue — which is what the compliance work actually consists of. But an audit is not legal advice, and nobody honest can promise you immunity from a claim. Be wary of anyone who does.

Do you fix the issues as well?

Not at these prices — we audit and advise, and the report is written so your existing developer can act on it directly. If you would rather we implemented the fixes, we can quote separately once we have seen what your site is built on.

Do I need this if I only sell in the UK?

The Equality Act 2010 applies to you either way. The European Accessibility Act becomes relevant on top of that if any EU consumer can buy from you.

My site was built recently by a good agency. Surely it is fine?

Usually not. Modern, well-built sites still routinely fail on colour contrast, form labelling, focus visibility and the newer WCAG 2.2 target-size rule. Take the guarantee: if there are fewer than five genuine issues, it costs you nothing to find out.

What exactly do I receive?

A written report — every issue, the exact element it affects, the WCAG success criterion, why it matters to a real user, and the fix. Plus a plain-English summary at the top for you, and a prioritised order to work through. On Standard and above, a drafted accessibility statement you can publish.
